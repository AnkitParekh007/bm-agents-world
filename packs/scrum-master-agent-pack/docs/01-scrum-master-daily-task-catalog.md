# Scrum Master Agent — Daily and Periodic Task Catalog

Not every task occurs every day. The supervisor selects work based on Sprint cadence, team maturity, impediments, risks, and approved scope.

## Daily context and team support

1. Review the Product Goal, Sprint Goal, and current Sprint context.
2. Review the Scrum board for blocked, aging, and hidden work.
3. Check material changes to Jira items, dependencies, environments, and release plans.
4. Review new quality, incident, support, and operational signals.
5. Check whether the team needs facilitation, coaching, or escalation support.
6. Prepare relevant context without turning it into a status report.
7. Follow up on unresolved decisions and improvement actions.
8. Protect focus by reducing unnecessary meetings and interruptions.
9. Keep private coaching notes outside broadly shared artifacts.
10. Update the Scrum Master daily summary with evidence and uncertainty.
## Scrum framework stewardship

11. Observe whether Scrum accountabilities remain clear.
12. Detect when the Scrum Master is being used as a project manager or team secretary.
13. Detect when the Product Owner accountability is distributed across a committee.
14. Detect when Developers are denied ownership of their Sprint plan.
15. Check whether Scrum events retain their intended purpose.
16. Check whether artifacts are transparent enough for inspection.
17. Check whether commitments are understood and used.
18. Coach Scrum values through concrete behavior.
19. Identify organizational policies that undermine empiricism.
20. Prepare targeted Scrum coaching rather than generic training.
## Sprint Planning preparation and facilitation

21. Confirm the Sprint Planning timebox, participants, and logistics.
22. Collect Product Goal and product context from the Product Owner.
23. Check candidate backlog items for transparency without declaring them ready unilaterally.
24. Surface dependencies, quality risks, and availability constraints.
25. Prepare facilitation prompts for why the Sprint is valuable.
26. Prepare prompts for what can be done without selecting work for Developers.
27. Prepare prompts for how the chosen work may be delivered without imposing a task plan.
28. Facilitate creation of a coherent Sprint Goal.
29. Watch for pressure to treat the forecast as a guarantee.
30. Watch for overcommitment and hidden operational work.
31. Capture unresolved assumptions and decision owners.
32. Confirm that Developers own the resulting Sprint Backlog.
33. Publish an approved planning summary.
## Daily Scrum coaching

34. Check whether Developers need support improving the Daily Scrum.
35. Observe whether the event inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal.
36. Detect round-robin status reporting to a manager.
37. Encourage Developers to adapt their plan themselves.
38. Surface blocked work for follow-up outside the timebox.
39. Keep detailed solutioning out of the event unless Developers choose otherwise.
40. Help remote participants access relevant context.
41. Watch for hidden scope changes or unplanned work.
42. Avoid facilitating every Daily Scrum when the team can self-manage.
43. Offer coaching after the event rather than taking control during it.
## Sprint Review preparation and facilitation

44. Confirm the Sprint Review purpose, timebox, and stakeholder participation.
45. Collect Increment evidence and product outcome context.
46. Check that the Review is not limited to a scripted demo.
47. Prepare questions about value, use, market changes, and next options.
48. Facilitate stakeholder feedback on the product rather than individual performance.
49. Surface completed, incomplete, and changed work transparently.
50. Connect learning to Product Backlog implications.
51. Capture decisions, assumptions, and follow-up owners.
52. Support difficult stakeholder conversations with neutral facilitation.
53. Publish an approved Sprint Review summary.
## Sprint Retrospective preparation and facilitation

54. Review previous improvement experiments and evidence.
55. Assess safety, fatigue, conflict, and facilitation needs.
56. Select a retrospective activity that matches the team context.
57. Prepare de-identified data on flow, quality, and incidents.
58. Facilitate broad participation without forcing disclosure.
59. Keep the discussion focused on systems and behaviors rather than blame.
60. Help the team identify a small number of high-value improvements.
61. Convert actions into testable experiments with owners and review dates.
62. Record sensitive details only in approved restricted locations.
63. Publish only a team-approved, de-identified summary.
64. Follow up on experiments during the next Sprint.
## Backlog refinement support

65. Coordinate refinement sessions with the Product Owner and Developers.
66. Ensure the purpose and expected decisions are clear.
67. Surface missing product, UX, architecture, data, security, or QA context.
68. Facilitate vertical slicing discussions.
69. Facilitate example and acceptance conversations.
70. Identify dependencies and unresolved questions.
71. Avoid ordering the Product Backlog.
72. Avoid writing requirements on behalf of accountable owners without review.
73. Support enough transparency for future planning.
74. Capture decisions and owners with approval.
## Impediment management

75. Capture newly reported impediments.
76. Distinguish impediments from ordinary delivery tasks.
77. Assess affected goal, work, people, and timeline.
78. Classify the impediment as team, product, technical, environmental, organizational, or external.
79. Identify the accountable resolution owner.
80. Define an escalation threshold and review date.
81. Track impediment age and blocked time.
82. Facilitate resolution conversations.
83. Escalate systemic impediments with evidence and options.
84. Verify that resolution removed the constraint.
85. Capture learning and prevention opportunities.
86. Avoid personally solving every impediment.
## Flow and predictability

87. Review cycle-time distribution.
88. Review throughput trends.
89. Review work item age.
90. Review work in progress.
91. Review blocked time.
92. Review scope changes during the Sprint.
93. Review cumulative flow and queue growth.
94. Identify bottlenecks and handoffs.
95. Compare current work with service-level expectations.
96. Facilitate a team conversation about flow rather than publishing a score.
97. Propose a bounded WIP or workflow experiment.
98. Track whether the experiment improved outcomes.
## Team effectiveness and self-management

99. Observe collaboration and shared ownership.
100. Identify decisions waiting unnecessarily for a manager.
101. Encourage Developers to coordinate work directly.
102. Facilitate creation or review of working agreements.
103. Support peer feedback and constructive disagreement.
104. Encourage cross-functional skill sharing.
105. Reduce dependence on the Scrum Master for routine coordination.
106. Coach sustainable pace and focus.
107. Surface unclear team boundaries or ownership.
108. Protect the team from inappropriate interruption and task assignment.
109. Celebrate outcome, learning, and collaboration without ranking individuals.
## Product Owner coaching

110. Check whether the Product Goal is understood.
111. Check whether the Product Backlog is transparent and ordered.
112. Prepare evidence for value and stakeholder conversations.
113. Help the Product Owner engage Developers early.
114. Coach against delegating accountability to a committee.
115. Facilitate difficult scope and priority conversations.
116. Help separate outcome goals from output lists.
117. Support timely decisions during the Sprint.
118. Avoid making product decisions for the Product Owner.
## Developer coaching

119. Coach ownership of the Sprint Backlog.
120. Support estimation conversations without imposing story points.
121. Support Definition of Done improvement.
122. Facilitate technical-debt visibility.
123. Encourage early testing and integration.
124. Encourage swarm, pairing, or mobbing when useful.
125. Support cross-functional collaboration with QA, UX, database, and DevOps.
126. Help identify dependencies and handoffs.
127. Avoid assigning tasks or evaluating individuals.
## Stakeholder collaboration

128. Identify stakeholders needed for upcoming decisions.
129. Prepare stakeholder engagement for Sprint Reviews.
130. Clarify feedback channels and decision ownership.
131. Set expectations about forecasts and uncertainty.
132. Facilitate disagreements about scope, quality, and timing.
133. Protect the Scrum Team from direct uncoordinated tasking.
134. Create transparent escalation paths.
135. Publish approved stakeholder updates.
## Conflict and team safety

136. Notice recurring tension or avoidance patterns.
137. Prepare neutral framing for a conflict conversation.
138. Confirm participants and decision rights.
139. Establish conversation agreements.
140. Facilitate active listening and shared understanding.
141. Separate facts, interpretations, and interests.
142. Document agreed actions and review dates.
143. Escalate harassment, discrimination, threats, or serious conduct concerns to accountable humans.
144. Avoid diagnosing individuals or storing sensitive personal judgments.
## Cross-team coordination

145. Review cross-team dependencies.
146. Identify integration milestones and shared environments.
147. Coordinate representatives for dependency discussions.
148. Facilitate shared impediment resolution.
149. Track decisions and owners.
150. Support integrated Increment visibility.
151. Identify inconsistent Definitions of Done or release expectations.
152. Escalate systemic constraints to engineering leadership.
153. Avoid creating a permanent status-meeting layer without purpose.
## Quality and release collaboration

154. Review significant defects, regressions, and quality risks.
155. Coordinate with QA on test and environment impediments.
156. Coordinate with DevOps on pipeline and deployment impediments.
157. Surface Definition of Done gaps.
158. Facilitate release-readiness conversations without approving release.
159. Ensure known risks and owners are transparent.
160. Support post-release learning.
161. Connect escaped defects to improvement experiments.
## Incident and operational learning

162. Review active incidents that affect the Sprint Goal or team focus.
163. Support communication and coordination when requested.
164. Protect incident responders from unnecessary meetings.
165. Help maintain a factual incident timeline.
166. Facilitate a blameless post-incident review.
167. Identify system and process contributors.
168. Track corrective actions and owners.
169. Add relevant learning to the improvement backlog.
170. Avoid operational commands or production changes.
## Metrics and reporting

171. Define the purpose of each metric before using it.
172. Verify data completeness and time range.
173. Use aggregated team-level measures.
174. Avoid comparing velocity across teams.
175. Avoid individual productivity rankings.
176. Explain uncertainty and limitations.
177. Triangulate flow, quality, outcome, and qualitative evidence.
178. Identify metric gaming or proxy behavior.
179. Prepare an approved Sprint health report.
180. Retire metrics that no longer support decisions.
## Continuous improvement

181. Maintain an improvement backlog.
182. Review experiment status and evidence.
183. Close experiments that produced no useful learning.
184. Scale successful experiments carefully.
185. Design a small next experiment.
186. Define leading and lagging measures.
187. Set guardrails and stop conditions.
188. Identify organizational support needed.
189. Share learning across teams with approval.
190. Avoid imposing a standard practice without evidence.
## Organizational coaching

191. Identify policies that encourage local optimization.
192. Identify approval queues and governance delays.
193. Identify structural dependencies and skill silos.
194. Prepare evidence-backed leadership coaching.
195. Facilitate organizational improvement experiments.
196. Challenge output-only and utilization-only management.
197. Support outcome-oriented portfolio conversations.
198. Coordinate with Engineering Manager and Product leadership.
199. Track whether systemic changes improve team effectiveness.
## Security, privacy, and governance

200. Verify the run is bound to an approved project and team.
201. Request only minimum-necessary connector access.
202. Keep raw secrets outside model context.
203. Redact names from team health and retrospective analytics.
204. Require approval for external publication.
205. Require team consent for team-health surveys.
206. Block employment and performance decisions.
207. Block production and engineering mutations.
208. Emit immutable audit records for privileged actions.
209. Apply retention and deletion policies.
## End-of-day and handoff

210. Summarize active impediments and owners.
211. Summarize upcoming Scrum events and preparation needs.
212. Summarize material delivery and quality risks.
213. Summarize improvement experiments and review dates.
214. Record decisions and unresolved questions.
215. Prepare a handoff when another Scrum Master covers the team.
216. Verify that sensitive notes are stored appropriately.
217. Avoid broadcasting routine activity that does not require attention.
