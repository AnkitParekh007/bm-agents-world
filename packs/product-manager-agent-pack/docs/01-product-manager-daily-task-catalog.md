# Product Manager — Complete Daily Task Catalog

Total catalogued tasks: **222**

Not every task is performed every day. The supervisor selects a bounded subset based on project, product lifecycle, evidence, decision authority, environment, and approved workflow.

## Work intake and daily planning

1. Open assigned Jira ideas, epics, stories, bugs, decisions, and discovery items.
2. Review product objectives, sprint goals, roadmap themes, release milestones, and current priorities.
3. Read new stakeholder requests and classify them as problems, opportunities, constraints, defects, or solution requests.
4. Review changes to scope, acceptance criteria, design, technical assumptions, and dependencies.
5. Review customer escalations, support themes, sales feedback, and operational incidents.
6. Review analytics alerts, experiment status, adoption trends, quality signals, and data-quality warnings.
7. Check blocked decisions, overdue actions, unresolved approvals, and upcoming deadlines.
8. Prioritize the day across discovery, strategy, delivery, release, communication, and outcome review.
9. Confirm the project, product area, environment, decision owner, and intended workflow for each task.
10. Update the personal and team product work queue with realistic next actions.

## Product and business context

11. Read the product vision, strategy, objectives, business model, target segments, and current roadmap.
12. Review organizational goals, financial constraints, contractual commitments, regulatory obligations, and service-level expectations.
13. Review previous product decisions, rejected alternatives, assumptions, and review triggers.
14. Review current architecture, supported platforms, environments, integrations, and operational constraints at a product level.
15. Review design-system, accessibility, localization, security, privacy, and data-governance constraints.
16. Review product lifecycle status: discovery, growth, maturity, maintenance, migration, or sunset.

18. Identify whether the request affects existing commitments, customers, partners, or internal operations.

20. Document context gaps that must be resolved before a recommendation.

## Customer discovery and insight management

21. Review approved interview notes, research findings, support cases, surveys, feedback portals, and customer advisory inputs.
22. Group feedback by user segment, job, workflow, pain point, frequency, severity, and business impact.
23. Separate direct evidence from interpretation, anecdote, stakeholder opinion, and untested assumption.

## Problem framing and opportunity discovery

43. Write a clear problem statement using user, context, need, impact, and evidence.
44. Separate symptoms, root causes, constraints, requests, and proposed solutions.
45. Map affected users, roles, jobs, workflows, triggers, and desired outcomes.

## Prioritization and tradeoffs

67. Normalize candidate work into comparable problem, outcome, scope, and evidence descriptions.
68. Select an appropriate prioritization method such as RICE, WSJF, value-effort, cost of delay, or custom criteria.
69. Score reach, impact, confidence, effort, strategic fit, urgency, risk reduction, and evidence strength.
70. Distinguish mandatory work from discretionary investment.
71. Identify dependencies, sequencing constraints, and enabling work.
72. Assess cost of delay, opportunity cost, and displaced work.
73. Run sensitivity analysis on uncertain scores and weights.
74. Check for double-counting, score gaming, loudest-voice bias, and false precision.
75. Balance customer value, business value, platform health, maintenance, compliance, and learning.
76. Create multiple capacity and portfolio scenarios.
77. Document tradeoffs and why lower-priority work is deferred.
78. Prepare a decision memo and request human prioritization approval.

## Agent autonomy classification

### Safe without publication approval

- Read authorized product context and privacy-thresholded aggregate analytics.
- Draft product artifacts in an isolated workspace.
- Calculate prioritization scenarios using declared inputs.
- Identify gaps, risks, dependencies, and questions.
- Produce read-only release and outcome summaries.

### Human approval required

- Create or update Jira/Jira Product Discovery items.
- Publish roadmaps, priorities, Confluence pages, or Teams messages.
- Contact customers or prospects.
- Launch surveys, betas, experiments, or customer communications.
- Change shared metric definitions or commercial assumptions.

### Prohibited

- Make roadmap, date, pricing, contractual, legal, or SLA commitments.
- Approve production releases.
- Change production flags, data, deployments, entitlements, or billing.
- Export raw customer, participant, sales, or commercial records to the language model.
- Fabricate customer quotes, analytics, market evidence, or experiment results.
