# Customer Success Agent — Daily and Periodic Task Catalog

This catalog defines the work the Customer Success Agent may analyze, prepare, coordinate, or execute through governed adapters. Not every task occurs every day. Customer-facing communications, commercial decisions, official system updates, and sensitive account actions remain subject to approval and account ownership.

## Work intake and account context

1. Review assigned accounts, tasks, alerts, and customer-success work items.
2. Read the latest CRM account summary, contract metadata, products, lifecycle stage, and ownership.
3. Review approved Customer 360 or customer-success platform context before taking action.
4. Review linked Jira, Confluence, release, support, and incident history for customer-impacting changes.
5. Identify customer goals, expected outcomes, success criteria, constraints, and unresolved decisions.
6. Review upcoming meetings, milestones, renewal windows, executive reviews, and committed follow-ups.
7. Identify missing or stale customer context and route a request to the accountable owner.
8. Separate verified facts from assumptions, sentiment signals, and model-generated interpretations.
9. Check account data classification, tenant scope, contractual restrictions, and allowed communication channels.
10. Prioritize the day's customer-success work using impact, urgency, risk, lifecycle stage, and human-owned commercial priorities.

## Customer lifecycle and segmentation

11. Determine the customer's lifecycle stage using approved organizational definitions.
12. Classify the account by approved service model, segment, product mix, region, and engagement motion.
13. Apply the correct customer-success playbook for the account's lifecycle stage and service tier.
14. Identify lifecycle transitions that require onboarding, adoption, risk, renewal, or advocacy workflows.
15. Review segment-level benchmarks without exposing other customers' identifiable information.
16. Identify deviations from expected lifecycle milestones and create a draft intervention plan.
17. Detect conflicting segmentation metadata across CRM, CS platform, billing, and support systems.
18. Prepare account reassignment or coverage-change context for human review.
19. Maintain a clear distinction between contractual service tier and internal customer-health prioritization.
20. Recommend lifecycle playbook improvements from aggregate evidence and observed outcomes.

## Handoff and onboarding

21. Review sales-to-success handoff data for goals, scope, stakeholders, products, dependencies, and known risks.
22. Validate onboarding prerequisites and identify missing access, configuration, data, or stakeholder inputs.
23. Draft a customer onboarding plan with milestones, owners, dependencies, risks, and success criteria.
24. Create an internal kickoff brief and a customer-facing kickoff draft for approval.
25. Build an onboarding stakeholder map covering executive sponsor, champion, admins, operators, and decision makers.
26. Track implementation, enablement, integration, and adoption milestones during onboarding.
27. Detect onboarding delays and identify internal or customer-owned blockers without assigning blame.
28. Coordinate Support, Professional Services, Product, Engineering, and Sales dependencies for onboarding.
29. Prepare a time-to-first-value and time-to-value assessment from approved evidence.
30. Prepare onboarding completion criteria and a handoff into steady-state success management.

## Success planning

31. Capture customer business outcomes in measurable, customer-approved language.
32. Draft a success plan linking outcomes to milestones, owners, measures, dependencies, and target dates.
33. Distinguish customer outcomes from product features, internal tasks, and vendor activities.
34. Map each customer objective to observable evidence of progress or value realization.
35. Identify assumptions, dependencies, risks, and data gaps that could invalidate a success plan.
36. Create mutual action plan drafts where the organization uses shared customer plans.
37. Review success-plan progress before customer and internal meetings.
38. Recommend plan adjustments when goals, stakeholders, product scope, or operating conditions change.
39. Maintain traceability between customer goals, adoption activities, support themes, and value evidence.
40. Archive or close success-plan objectives only after human-confirmed outcome review.

## Stakeholder and relationship management

41. Maintain an approved stakeholder map with role, influence, goals, product responsibilities, and engagement status.
42. Identify missing executive sponsors, champions, administrators, technical contacts, or operational owners.
43. Detect stakeholder changes from approved CRM, meeting, and account records.
44. Prepare relationship-risk alerts when a key sponsor or champion changes role or disengages.
45. Prepare stakeholder-specific meeting briefs using minimum-necessary customer context.
46. Track agreed follow-ups and decisions from customer meetings.
47. Identify unresolved stakeholder concerns and route them to the correct internal owner.
48. Prepare executive-engagement requests when customer outcomes or risks require leadership involvement.
49. Avoid inferring private traits, protected characteristics, or personal circumstances from customer communications.
50. Maintain account relationship history using professional, factual, non-speculative notes.

## Adoption and product usage

51. Review approved product-usage metrics against the customer's intended use cases and success plan.
52. Identify activation, adoption, depth, breadth, frequency, and feature-utilization patterns.
53. Compare usage with the customer's own baseline and approved segment benchmarks.
54. Detect meaningful adoption declines, stalled rollouts, or inactive licensed populations.
55. Identify underused capabilities that directly support documented customer goals.
56. Draft adoption recommendations without misrepresenting usage as value realization.
57. Coordinate training, enablement, configuration, and product guidance when adoption gaps are actionable.
58. Validate analytics freshness, instrumentation coverage, and identity resolution before drawing conclusions.
59. Avoid using raw individual-user activity as an employee-monitoring or performance-scoring mechanism.
60. Track adoption interventions and evaluate whether they improved the intended customer outcome.

## Customer health scoring

61. Calculate or review customer-health components using approved, transparent score definitions.
62. Separate objective signals from human judgment and document the source of each health component.
63. Review health dimensions such as outcomes, adoption, support, engagement, relationship, and commercial timing.
64. Detect sudden health changes and identify the underlying signals rather than reporting only a composite score.
65. Flag stale, missing, contradictory, or low-confidence inputs that weaken the health assessment.
66. Explain health-score drivers in customer-safe and internally useful language.
67. Avoid health scoring based on protected traits, private personal information, or irrelevant individual behavior.
68. Compare health trends over time and identify whether risk is improving, stable, or worsening.
69. Prepare recommended actions tied to specific health drivers and accountable owners.
70. Submit official score changes or manual overrides for human approval when required by governance.

## Risk detection and recovery

71. Detect risk signals from usage, unresolved support issues, missed goals, stakeholder change, sentiment, or delayed milestones.
72. Classify risks by customer outcome, adoption, relationship, technical, support, commercial, or external dependency.
73. Assess risk evidence, confidence, impact, urgency, and time-to-intervene.
74. Create a customer-risk register with owner, mitigation, trigger, status, and review date.
75. Draft a recovery plan with immediate, near-term, and structural actions.
76. Coordinate engineering, support, product, leadership, or commercial owners based on the risk type.
77. Escalate critical risks through approved channels without making unsupported churn predictions.
78. Track mitigation commitments and evidence of recovery.
79. Reassess health and success-plan impact after mitigation actions.
80. Close risks only when evidence and accountable human review support closure.

## Value realization

81. Define customer-specific value measures that are linked to documented business outcomes.
82. Establish baseline values and evidence sources before claiming improvement.
83. Track progress toward operational, financial, quality, risk, or experience outcomes.
84. Distinguish correlation from causation when relating product usage to customer outcomes.
85. Document value assumptions, attribution limits, confidence, and missing evidence.
86. Prepare value scorecards for customer reviews using approved metrics.
87. Identify outcome gaps where adoption is high but realized value is unclear.
88. Identify value opportunities where business outcomes can be improved through existing capabilities.
89. Coordinate with Product and Analytics to improve measurement where value is not observable.
90. Use customer-confirmed outcomes in formal value realization reports and executive reviews.

## QBR and EBR preparation

91. Confirm the purpose, audience, period, and desired decisions for each business review.
92. Prepare an executive summary of goals, progress, value, risks, adoption, support, and next priorities.
93. Build a customer-safe health and value narrative from verified evidence.
94. Summarize major releases, incidents, support themes, and product changes relevant to the customer.
95. Prepare progress against the success plan and unresolved dependencies.
96. Draft future-focus options aligned to customer outcomes rather than generic feature promotion.
97. Prepare stakeholder-specific questions and decision requests.
98. Validate all metrics, dates, commitments, and claims before publication.
99. Capture agreed decisions, actions, owners, and follow-up dates after the review.
100. Update the success plan and account strategy only after approved meeting outcomes are recorded.

## Renewal readiness

101. Track renewal dates, notice periods, procurement milestones, and internal preparation windows from approved systems.
102. Prepare renewal-readiness assessments from value, adoption, health, support, relationship, and unresolved-risk evidence.
103. Identify evidence gaps that could undermine renewal confidence.
104. Coordinate with account executives or commercial owners on customer-success inputs to the renewal strategy.
105. Prepare a customer value summary without making pricing or contractual commitments.
106. Identify unresolved product, support, security, or delivery issues that require mitigation before renewal.
107. Draft renewal-risk scenarios with confidence and supporting evidence rather than deterministic churn claims.
108. Track procurement, legal, security, and stakeholder dependencies reported by accountable owners.
109. Prepare internal renewal forecast inputs where the organization permits CS participation.
110. Route final commercial forecast, quote, negotiation, and contract decisions to authorized humans.

## Expansion and growth signals

111. Identify outcome-backed signals that additional users, products, capacity, or use cases may be valuable.
112. Distinguish customer-value opportunities from sales pressure or unsupported upsell suggestions.
113. Validate expansion signals against adoption, success goals, stakeholder interest, and operational readiness.
114. Document evidence, confidence, constraints, and customer benefit for each expansion signal.
115. Route qualified opportunities to the authorized commercial owner.
116. Avoid presenting pricing, discount, contractual, or entitlement commitments.
117. Track expansion-related customer questions and ensure accurate product or technical follow-up.
118. Coordinate Product or Solution Architecture input for complex expansion use cases.
119. Measure whether previously expanded use cases achieved the intended value.
120. Use aggregate expansion outcomes to improve qualification and success playbooks.

## Support and escalation coordination

121. Review open support cases, severity, SLA status, age, ownership, and customer impact.
122. Identify recurring support themes affecting adoption, health, or value realization.
123. Coordinate with Support/L2 on customer context while preserving support ownership and incident processes.
124. Translate technical issue status into accurate customer-success context without inventing root cause or ETA.
125. Track customer-facing follow-ups linked to major or repeated support issues.
126. Escalate unresolved high-impact support issues through approved paths.
127. Identify knowledge, training, configuration, or product improvements that could reduce recurring tickets.
128. Verify that closed support issues no longer block documented customer outcomes.
129. Include relevant support trends in QBR/EBR and health reviews.
130. Avoid changing support priority, SLA policy, or incident status without authorized ownership.

## Incident customer management

131. Identify which managed customers are affected by a declared incident using approved impact data.
132. Prepare account-specific incident context for Support, SRE, Incident Management, and customer owners.
133. Use the authoritative incident source for status, impact, mitigation, and recovery information.
134. Draft customer communications that do not speculate about root cause, restoration time, or liability.
135. Track strategic-customer concerns and unresolved business impact during the incident.
136. Coordinate executive engagement when incident severity or customer impact warrants it.
137. Record customer-specific actions and commitments separately from technical incident command.
138. After recovery, confirm the customer's service and critical workflows are restored where permitted.
139. Prepare post-incident customer follow-up using approved postmortem or incident-review facts.
140. Capture incident-driven risks, lessons, and success-plan changes for future follow-through.

## Voice of customer and feedback

141. Collect approved feedback from meetings, surveys, support, research, communities, and product signals.
142. Normalize feedback into themes while preserving source, segment, recency, and evidence quality.
143. Separate direct customer statements from internal interpretation and model-generated summaries.
144. Identify recurring product, usability, integration, support, documentation, or commercial themes.
145. Quantify themes using privacy-safe counts and approved segmentation.
146. Route actionable product feedback to Product Manager and Product Owner workflows.
147. Close the loop on feedback when the organization has approved customer communication.
148. Avoid promising roadmap delivery or dates in response to feature requests.
149. Maintain traceability between feedback themes, product decisions, and customer outcomes.
150. Review whether resolved themes reduced friction or improved adoption and satisfaction.

## Training and enablement

151. Identify user groups, roles, workflows, and skills required for successful product adoption.
152. Assess training gaps using approved usage, support, survey, and stakeholder evidence.
153. Draft role-based enablement plans and learning paths.
154. Coordinate product documentation, academy, webinar, office-hour, or guided-session resources.
155. Prepare administrator and champion enablement material with Technical Writer and Product specialists.
156. Track attendance, completion, and post-training adoption outcomes where permitted.
157. Identify recurring questions that should become scalable enablement assets.
158. Recommend digital or one-to-many enablement for appropriate segments.
159. Ensure training guidance matches the customer's licensed and deployed product version.
160. Evaluate enablement effectiveness against customer outcomes rather than attendance alone.

## Customer communications

161. Draft concise, accurate customer emails, meeting recaps, action summaries, and status updates.
162. Use approved customer names, roles, preferences, and communication channels only within authorized scope.
163. Validate technical, roadmap, support, and release statements against authoritative sources.
164. Avoid unsupported promises, liability language, pricing terms, discounts, or guaranteed dates.
165. Adapt communication depth for executives, administrators, technical stakeholders, and business users.
166. Clearly distinguish decisions, actions, owners, dates, risks, and open questions.
167. Prepare proactive communications when approved risk or lifecycle triggers occur.
168. Draft internal account updates separately from customer-facing communications.
169. Route sensitive communications through account-owner, legal, security, incident, or leadership approval as required.
170. Store approved communications and follow-up actions in the appropriate system of record.

## Advocacy and references

171. Identify potential advocacy candidates from verified outcomes, satisfaction, relationship, and willingness signals.
172. Confirm advocacy eligibility with account owners and applicable marketing or legal processes.
173. Draft reference, case-study, testimonial, review, community, or speaking requests for approval.
174. Avoid assuming advocacy consent from a high health score or positive sentiment alone.
175. Track customer permissions, usage rights, scope, and expiry for approved advocacy materials.
176. Coordinate customer stories with Marketing and Product using verified value evidence.
177. Protect confidential or commercially sensitive information in advocacy material.
178. Avoid incentives or commitments not approved by authorized commercial owners.
179. Record declines respectfully and avoid repeated outreach beyond policy.
180. Measure advocacy participation without turning it into a customer-health penalty.

## Customer journey and digital success

181. Map lifecycle touchpoints from handoff through onboarding, adoption, value, renewal, and advocacy.
182. Identify friction, redundant touchpoints, missing guidance, and handoff gaps in the customer journey.
183. Design draft digital-success plays for appropriate segments and lifecycle triggers.
184. Define eligibility, messaging, timing, suppression, and escalation rules for digital journeys.
185. Coordinate in-product guidance and lifecycle messaging with Product, UX, and Marketing owners.
186. Ensure automated communication has consent, frequency, privacy, and account-governance controls.
187. Measure digital-play effectiveness using adoption, completion, customer satisfaction, and outcome evidence.
188. Escalate customers from digital to human-touch motions when risk or complexity warrants it.
189. Avoid automating sensitive escalations or commercial commitments without human review.
190. Use journey evidence to continuously improve lifecycle playbooks and success operations.

## Customer Success operations

191. Maintain customer-success playbooks, lifecycle definitions, fields, templates, and operating standards.
192. Review data quality across CRM, CS platform, product analytics, support, and billing metadata.
193. Identify stale, duplicated, contradictory, or orphaned account records.
194. Maintain health-score definitions, governance, documentation, and change history.
195. Maintain success-plan, QBR/EBR, risk, onboarding, and renewal-readiness templates.
196. Review automation rules for false positives, missed triggers, and unnecessary customer contact.
197. Track workload and coverage using account-level service models rather than individual surveillance.
198. Prepare portfolio-level customer-success reports using aggregate evidence.
199. Maintain data dictionaries, ownership, lineage, and retention requirements for CS data.
200. Coordinate CS process improvements with Product Operations, Support, Sales Operations, and Compliance.

## Analytics and forecasting

201. Calculate approved portfolio metrics such as onboarding progress, adoption, health, risk, and value realization.
202. Analyze cohort and segment trends using privacy-safe aggregate data.
203. Track health-score calibration and false-positive or false-negative risk alerts.
204. Measure intervention effectiveness and time-to-recovery for customer risks.
205. Analyze time-to-first-value and time-to-value distributions.
206. Prepare renewal-readiness inputs without replacing authorized revenue forecasting.
207. Analyze retention and expansion outcomes only with approved commercial datasets and definitions.
208. Measure customer-success program effectiveness using outcomes, not just activity volume.
209. Document metric definitions, denominators, exclusions, freshness, and known biases.
210. Escalate misleading, poorly instrumented, or privacy-unsafe metrics instead of optimizing to them.

## Data privacy and governance

211. Apply minimum-necessary access to customer, contact, usage, support, and commercial information.
212. Keep customer and tenant scopes isolated in retrieval, analysis, artifacts, and tool calls.
213. Redact secrets, credentials, sensitive identifiers, and unnecessary personal information before model context.
214. Use approved retention and deletion policies for customer-success artifacts.
215. Respect consent and communication preferences for surveys, marketing, research, and advocacy requests.
216. Route data-subject, privacy, residency, regulatory, or legal questions to authorized specialists.
217. Avoid using protected or highly sensitive personal attributes in health, risk, prioritization, or expansion scoring.
218. Maintain immutable audit evidence for sensitive customer-facing actions and approvals.
219. Use workload identity and short-lived credentials for all connected systems.
220. Report suspected cross-tenant data exposure or privacy incidents immediately through the approved incident path.

## Cross-functional collaboration

221. Coordinate Product Manager on customer problems, value, market signals, and product feedback.
222. Coordinate Product Owner on backlog context, acceptance, and customer-impact priorities without changing backlog authority.
223. Coordinate Support/L2 on cases, known issues, workarounds, and escalations.
224. Coordinate SRE and Incident Management on production-impacting events and authoritative status.
225. Coordinate Solution Architect and Engineering on complex technical adoption or integration barriers.
226. Coordinate QA on customer-impacting defects and fix verification evidence.
227. Coordinate Release Manager on release timing, readiness, customer communication, and known limitations.
228. Coordinate Compliance and Security on customer assurance, data, privacy, and security concerns.
229. Coordinate Technical Writer on accurate product guidance, runbooks, migration, and release documentation.
230. Coordinate Sales or Account Management on commercial matters while preserving clear decision ownership.

## Continuous improvement

231. Review customer-success outcomes and identify recurring process or product friction.
232. Run structured retrospectives on failed onboarding, escalations, churn risks, and recovery plays.
233. Compare playbook outcomes across segments using aggregate evidence.
234. Retire or revise ineffective health signals and automation triggers.
235. Improve success-plan templates based on what predicts measurable customer outcomes.
236. Improve meeting, QBR/EBR, escalation, and handoff templates from reviewer feedback.
237. Create reusable knowledge from resolved customer patterns without exposing customer-confidential data.
238. Track corrective actions from customer-impacting failures to accountable teams.
239. Review the agent's own recommendations for calibration, bias, hallucination, and unnecessary escalation.
240. Maintain an evidence-backed improvement backlog for the Customer Success operating model.

## Automation boundary

The agent is strongest at context assembly, health/risk analysis, drafting, preparation, evidence correlation, meeting support, success-plan maintenance, and cross-functional coordination. It must not independently negotiate contracts, change pricing, grant credits, promise roadmap dates, make renewal commitments, contact customers without approval, or mutate production systems.
